A Residential Project Inspired by Culture, Craft, and Timeless Design

Aug
2025

The story behind the space

The stair was the first thing we drew and the last thing we finished. It sits in the middle of the plan, which meant it had to work as circulation, as a light well, and as the piece of the apartment you see from the front door — three jobs that usually pull in different directions.

The plaster panels came out of a conversation with a workshop in Sayeda Zeinab that still cuts relief by hand. We took the lotus and papyrus motifs from a set of 1930s Cairo apartment blocks a few streets away, redrew them at a scale that would read from the landing rather than up close, and had them cast in sections so the wall could be assembled dry.

Underfoot, the runner is a wool pile woven in a soft camouflage pattern — deliberately quiet, so the wall carries the room. The balustrade is black steel with brass ball detailing, thin enough to disappear against the window and heavy enough to feel safe under the hand.

Lighting is buried in the stringer, washing each tread from the side. There are no downlights over the stair at all, which keeps the relief panels lit from the front and stops the whole thing turning into a set of hard shadows in the evening.

What we like most about it is the thing you can’t photograph: the way the light off the river moves across the plaster through the afternoon, so a wall that looks flat at noon has depth by five.
